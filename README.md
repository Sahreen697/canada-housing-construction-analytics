# Canada Housing Construction Analytics

## Project Overview

This project analyzes housing construction trends across Canadian provinces using Statistics Canada open data. The dashboard explores housing activity by province, dwelling type, market segment, and time period to identify trends related to housing supply and development across Canada.

## Business Problem

Canada is experiencing a housing affordability crisis driven by population growth, rising housing costs, and increasing demand for both ownership and rental housing. While housing construction continues across the country, it is important to understand not only how much housing is being built, but also where it is being built, what types of housing are being developed, and which market segments are being served.

This project analyzes housing construction trends across Canadian provinces using Statistics Canada data to evaluate whether current development patterns align with housing affordability needs. The analysis explores dwelling types, market segments, and regional construction activity to identify opportunities for more balanced and affordable housing development.

## Dashboard Features

- Housing Starts Analysis
- Provincial Comparison
- Dwelling Type Analysis
- Market Segment Analysis
- Time-Series trend Trends

## Key Insights

- Ontario contributed the largest share of housing construction activity in Canada.
- Apartments represented the largest dwelling category, highlighting the shift toward higher-density housing.
- Homeowner developments accounted for nearly half of all housing starts, while rental housing represented a significantly smaller share.
- Construction activity was concentrated in Ontario, Quebec, British Columbia, and Alberta.
- Several smaller provinces recorded substantially lower housing construction activity compared to larger urbanized regions.
- Current construction patterns suggest that housing supply growth may not fully align with increasing demand for affordable rental housing.
